Citizens Bank — BSA/AML Compliance & KYC Program

OVERVIEW

This document defines Citizens Bank's Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) compliance program, Customer Identification Program (CIP), Know Your Customer (KYC) procedures, OFAC sanctions screening, and Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) / Currency Transaction Report (CTR) filing requirements.

BSA/AML PROGRAM

1. PROGRAM GOVERNANCE

1.1 BSA Officer
- A designated BSA/AML Compliance Officer is responsible for day-to-day program oversight
- Reports directly to the Chief Compliance Officer and Board of Directors
- Authority to file SARs independently and escalate to law enforcement

1.2 Board Oversight
- Board receives quarterly AML program updates
- Annual BSA/AML risk assessment reviewed and approved by the Board
- Independent audit of BSA/AML program conducted annually

1.3 Four Pillars
- Written policies, procedures, and controls
- Designated compliance officer with sufficient authority
- Ongoing employee training program
- Independent testing/audit of the program

2. CUSTOMER IDENTIFICATION PROGRAM (CIP)

2.1 Minimum Information Required — Individuals
- Full legal name (as appears on government-issued ID)
- Date of birth
- Address (residential street address; PO Box acceptable only if street address also provided)
- Identification number (SSN for US persons; passport/ITIN for non-US persons)

2.2 Minimum Information Required — Entities
- Legal name of entity
- Principal place of business address
- EIN or taxpayer identification number
- Beneficial ownership information (25% ownership threshold per FinCEN CDD Rule)

2.3 Identity Verification Methods
- Documentary: government-issued photo ID (driver's license, passport, state ID)
- Non-documentary: credit bureau verification, knowledge-based authentication (KBA)
- All new accounts require verification within 30 days of account opening
- Expired IDs not accepted; must be valid at time of verification

2.4 Customer Due Diligence (CDD)
- Standard Due Diligence: all retail and business customers
- Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD): high-risk customers, PEPs, high-risk jurisdictions
- EDD triggers: high AML risk rating, PEP status, sanctions hits, adverse media, large cash usage

3. KNOW YOUR CUSTOMER (KYC)

3.1 Risk Rating Categories
- Low Risk: US-based, stable income, standard products, no adverse history
- Medium Risk: complex ownership, international connections, moderate transaction volumes
- High Risk: PEPs, high-value cash transactions, high-risk countries, adverse media
- Very High Risk: sanctions hits, confirmed fraud, or linked to criminal activity

3.2 Ongoing Monitoring
- Low risk: annual review
- Medium risk: semi-annual review
- High risk: quarterly review with manager sign-off
- Very high risk: monthly review; escalation to BSA Officer required

3.3 Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)
- PEPs defined as current/former senior government officials and their immediate family
- Automatic EDD required for all PEPs
- Source of funds and source of wealth documentation mandatory
- Senior management approval required to onboard or continue PEP relationships

4. OFAC SANCTIONS SCREENING

4.1 Screening Requirements
- All new customers screened against OFAC Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list at onboarding
- Ongoing screening: all existing customers re-screened within 24 hours of SDN list updates
- All wire transfers screened before execution
- International transactions screened against country-level sanctions programs

4.2 Hit Resolution
- Potential hits generate automatic account freeze pending review
- BSA team must clear or escalate within 1 business day
- Confirmed hits: account blocked, assets frozen, OFAC report filed within 10 business days
- False positives documented and cleared with supervisor sign-off

5. TRANSACTION MONITORING

5.1 Automated System Rules (key examples)
- Cash transactions ≥ $10,000 in a single day (CTR trigger)
- Multiple cash transactions totaling ≥ $10,000 structured over consecutive days (structuring alert)
- Wire transfers to/from high-risk jurisdictions ≥ $5,000
- Rapid movement of funds: deposit + withdrawal of ≥ 80% within 5 business days
- Round-dollar transactions: multiple round-dollar amounts on same account in 24 hours
- New account activity: large transactions within 30 days of account opening

5.2 Alert Review
- Level 1 review: AML analyst within 5 business days
- Level 2 escalation: BSA Officer within 2 business days of Level 1 escalation
- 30-day clock for SAR filing begins on date the bank first knows of suspicious activity

6. SAR FILING REQUIREMENTS

6.1 Mandatory Filing Triggers
- Known or suspected criminal violation involving $5,000+ where bank is victim or conduit
- Transaction of $5,000+ involving funds from illegal activity
- Insider abuse of any amount
- Terrorism financing of any amount

6.2 Filing Process
- BSA Officer approves all SARs before submission
- Filed electronically via FinCEN BSA E-Filing System within 30 days of detection
- 60-day extension available if subject is unidentifiable
- SARs and supporting documentation retained for 5 years

6.3 SAR Confidentiality
- Strict prohibition on tipping off SAR subjects (18 U.S.C. § 1513)
- SAR existence may not be disclosed to any person outside the bank except FinCEN and law enforcement
- "No duty to disclose" protection under 31 U.S.C. § 5318(g)(2)

7. CURRENCY TRANSACTION REPORTS (CTR)

7.1 Filing Requirements
- Required for cash-in or cash-out transactions exceeding $10,000 in a single business day
- Multiple transactions by same person on same day must be aggregated
- Filed within 15 calendar days of transaction
- Retained for 5 years

7.2 CTR Exemptions
- Phase I: banks, government entities, listed public companies
- Phase II: non-listed businesses with established cash-intensive history
- Exemption review: annual for Phase II; must be revoked if suspicious activity detected

8. EMPLOYEE TRAINING

- All employees: annual BSA/AML awareness training (mandatory)
- Customer-facing staff: enhanced training on CIP and suspicious activity identification
- BSA/AML team: advanced training including FinCEN guidance and typologies
- New hires: BSA training within 30 days of start date

9. RECORD RETENTION

- CIP records: 5 years after account closure
- SAR records: 5 years from filing date
- CTR records: 5 years from filing date
- Transaction records: 5 years
- OFAC screening records: 5 years
